How to stop doom scrolling

You can’t delete the urge to scroll, but you can redirect it. Practical steps to break the doom scrolling habit, and how to turn the same thumb motion into time with the Quran.

In short: willpower alone rarely beats an app designed to keep you scrolling. Make scrolling harder, make the replacement easier, and keep the habit loop but change what is at the end of it.

Why it is so hard to stop

Doom scrolling isn’t a decision; it is a loop. A cue (boredom, stress, a spare moment) leads to a routine (pick up the phone, open the app, swipe) and a reward (something new, every few seconds). Feeds are designed so the reward never quite runs out. The loop runs dozens of times a day, mostly without you choosing it.

That is why “I’ll just use it less” rarely works. A habit loop is easier to redirect than to delete.

1. Notice the cue

For a few days, notice what happens just before you open the app. Waiting in line? Lying in bed? Avoiding something difficult? Knowing your cues tells you where to put the replacement.

2. Add friction to the feed

  • Move social apps off your home screen, into a folder on the last page.
  • Log out after each use, so opening the app takes effort.
  • Use Screen Time limits for the apps that pull you in most.
  • Keep the phone out of the bedroom, or at least out of reach of the bed.

3. Put something better where the feed was

This is the step most advice misses. Your thumb will still reach for the same spot on the screen. Put something there you are glad to find: for many Muslims, that is the Quran. When you open Quran Scroll, the motion is identical (swipe up, see something new), but each swipe is an ayah in Arabic and English. You keep the loop; you change the reward.

4. Make the replacement small

Don’t replace an hour of scrolling with an hour of reading. Replace one scroll session with five minutes of ayahs. Small enough that you will actually do it, every day. See how to read the Quran every day.

5. Protect the edges of your day

The first and last minutes of the day shape the rest of it. Many people find the easiest win is to read before touching any other app after Fajr, and to end the night with a surah like Al-Mulk instead of the feed.

6. Be gentle with yourself

You will slip. When you catch yourself twenty minutes into a feed, don’t spiral into guilt. Put it down, open something better, and carry on. The Quran describes Allah’s mercy as wider than any slip:

Az-Zumar 39:53

۞ قُلْ يَٰعِبَادِىَ ٱلَّذِينَ أَسْرَفُوا۟ عَلَىٰٓ أَنفُسِهِمْ لَا تَقْنَطُوا۟ مِن رَّحْمَةِ ٱللَّهِ ۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ يَغْفِرُ ٱلذُّنُوبَ جَمِيعًا ۚ إِنَّهُۥ هُوَ ٱلْغَفُورُ ٱلرَّحِيمُ

Qul yaa'ibaadiyal lazeena asrafoo 'alaaa anfusihim laa taqnatoo mirrahmatil laah; innal laaha yaghfiruz zunooba jamee'aa; innahoo Huwal Ghafoorur Raheem

Say, "O My servants who have transgressed against themselves [by sinning], do not despair of the mercy of Allah. Indeed, Allah forgives all sins. Indeed, it is He who is the Forgiving, the Merciful."

What the Quran says about time

A whole surah swears by time and warns that people are in loss, except those who believe, do good and remind each other of truth and patience. It is three ayahs long and takes less than a minute to read: Surah Al-‘Asr. For more, see Quran verses about time.
