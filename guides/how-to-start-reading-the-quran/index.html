How to start reading the Quran as a beginner

Where to start, which surahs to read first, whether to read in Arabic or English, and how to keep going. A calm, practical guide for anyone opening the Quran for the first time, or for the first time in a long time.

In short: you don’t have to start at page one. Begin with Al-Fatihah and a few short surahs, read the translation alongside the Arabic, and aim for a few minutes every day rather than a lot at once.

You don’t have to start at the beginning

The Quran isn’t arranged in the order it was revealed, and it isn’t a story you have to read from page one. Its second surah, Al-Baqarah, is also its longest, which is one reason many beginners stall early. It is completely fine to start with shorter surahs and come back.

Five good places to start

  1. Al-Fatihah (7 ayahs). The opening, recited in every prayer. Learn what you are saying.
  2. The last three surahs: Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq and An-Nas. Short, foundational, and recited for protection.
  3. Ad-Duha and Ash-Sharh. Two short surahs of reassurance, including “indeed, with hardship comes ease.”
  4. Al-Mulk (30 ayahs). About ten minutes, and a surah many people read every night.
  5. Yusuf. One complete story from start to finish, and an easy way to fall in love with reading the Quran.

Arabic, English, or both?

If you can read Arabic, read the Arabic and the translation side by side: the Arabic is the Quran itself, and the translation helps you understand it. If you can’t read Arabic yet, reading a translation is still a real way to connect with the Quran, and transliteration can help you begin to recite. All the surahs on this site show all three, with the Saheeh International translation.

Read a little, every day

Five minutes a day is better than an hour once a month. Read at the same time each day, ideally after a prayer, and stop while you still want to keep going. Our guide on reading the Quran every day has more on making it stick.

When an ayah stops you, stop

The goal isn’t to get through pages. When an ayah moves you, read it again, look at the words, and ask what it means for your life today. The first word revealed was an instruction to read:

Al-'Alaq 96:1

ٱقْرَأْ بِٱسْمِ رَبِّكَ ٱلَّذِى خَلَقَ

Iqra bismi rab bikal lazee khalaq

Recite in the name of your Lord who created -

Find the ayahs for your life right now

A powerful way in is to start with what you are going through: anxiety, loneliness, gratitude, a big decision. Or describe it in your own words and we’ll find ayahs that speak to it.

Don’t do it alone

A teacher, a study circle at your local mosque, or a friend reading alongside you will carry you through the days you don’t feel like it, and can answer questions about meaning that a translation can’t.
