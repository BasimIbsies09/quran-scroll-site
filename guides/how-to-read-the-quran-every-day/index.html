How to read the Quran every day

Start smaller than you think, tie it to something you already do, and track it. Seven practical habits for building a daily Quran routine that lasts past the first week.

In short: pick a goal so small you can’t fail (five minutes, or one page), attach it to a prayer you already pray, keep the Quran where your phone habit lives, and keep a streak.

Most people who decide to read the Quran daily start strong and stop within two weeks. That isn’t a lack of faith; it is how habits work. The Prophet ﷺ said: “The most beloved deeds to Allah are those done consistently, even if they are small” (Bukhari and Muslim). Here is how to make it consistent.

1. Start smaller than feels worthwhile

Five minutes a day, or a single page. It should feel almost too easy. You can always read more; what matters is that you never have a reason to skip. Five minutes a day is about two and a half pages, enough to finish the whole Quran in under a year (see the planner).

2. Attach it to a prayer

New habits stick best when they follow something you already do. Read straight after Fajr or Isha, before you get up from the prayer mat. The prayer becomes the reminder.

3. Put it where your thumb already goes

Most of us pick up our phones dozens of times a day without deciding to. That reflex is the most reliable habit you have, so point it at the Quran. Put a Quran app where your most-used social app sits on your home screen, and open it instead. That is the idea behind Quran Scroll: the same swipe, one ayah at a time. More on that in how to stop doom scrolling.

4. Keep a streak, and forgive a broken one

Marking each day you read makes progress visible and makes skipping feel like a cost. But when you miss a day, start again the next day without guilt. A streak is a tool, not a judgement.

5. Read for meaning, not just to finish

Pages read without understanding are hard to keep coming back to. Read the translation alongside the Arabic, and when an ayah stops you, stay with it. The Quran asks to be reflected on:

Sad 38:29

كِتَٰبٌ أَنزَلْنَٰهُ إِلَيْكَ مُبَٰرَكٌۭ لِّيَدَّبَّرُوٓا۟ ءَايَٰتِهِۦ وَلِيَتَذَكَّرَ أُو۟لُوا۟ ٱلْأَلْبَٰبِ

Kitaabun anzalnaahu ilaika mubaarakul liyaddabbarooo Aayaatihee wa liyatazakkara ulul albaab

[This is] a blessed Book which We have revealed to you, [O Muhammad], that they might reflect upon its verses and that those of understanding would be reminded.

6. Follow a plan

“Read more Quran” is a wish; “Surah Al-Kahf every Friday” or “Juz ‘Amma this month” is a plan. Pick one with an end date. Some good first plans: Ya-Sin over a week, Al-Mulk before sleep, Al-Kahf on Fridays, or a juz a day in Ramadan.

7. Let the Quran meet you where you are

On hard days, read ayahs that speak to what you are going through: anxiety, grief, hope, or describe your situation and find ayahs for it. You are far more likely to keep reading something that feels like it was written for today.

Ar-Ra'd 13:28

ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَتَطْمَئِنُّ قُلُوبُهُم بِذِكْرِ ٱللَّهِ ۗ أَلَا بِذِكْرِ ٱللَّهِ تَطْمَئِنُّ ٱلْقُلُوبُ

Allazeena aamanoo wa tatma'innu quloobuhum bizikril laah; alaa bizikril laahi tatma'innul quloob

Those who have believed and whose hearts are assured by the remembrance of Allah. Unquestionably, by the remembrance of Allah hearts are assured."
