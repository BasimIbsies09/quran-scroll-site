How many surahs, ayahs, juz and pages are in the Quran?

The Quran has 114 surahs, 6,236 ayahs, 30 juz and 604 pages in the standard Madani mushaf. Here are the numbers, the longest and shortest surahs, and how it is divided.

Surahs

The Quran has 114 surahs. 86 were revealed in Makkah and 28 in Madinah, according to the most common classification. Surahs are arranged roughly from longest to shortest, not in the order they were revealed. Every surah begins with the Bismillah except At-Tawbah (surah 9).

Ayahs

There are 6,236 ayahs in the count followed by most printed Qurans today (the Kufan count, from the recitation of Hafs from ‘Asim). Other classical counts differ slightly, because scholars counted the end of some ayahs differently, not because the text differs.

Juz, hizb and pages

For reading and memorisation, the Quran is divided into 30 juz (parts) of about equal length, each split into 2 hizb, giving 60. In the standard Madani mushaf each juz is about 20 pages, for 604 pages in total. Reading one juz a day finishes the Quran in a month, which is why many people do exactly that in Ramadan (see the khatam planner).

Longest surahs

  1. Al-Baqarah (surah 2): 286 ayahs
  2. Ash-Shu'ara (surah 26): 227 ayahs
  3. Al-A'raf (surah 7): 206 ayahs
  4. Ali 'Imran (surah 3): 200 ayahs
  5. As-Saffat (surah 37): 182 ayahs

Shortest surahs

  1. Al-Kawthar (surah 108): 3 ayahs
  2. Al-'Asr (surah 103): 3 ayahs
  3. An-Nasr (surah 110): 3 ayahs
  4. Al-Ikhlas (surah 112): 4 ayahs
  5. Quraysh (surah 106): 4 ayahs

Longest ayah

The longest ayah is Al-Baqarah 2:282, about recording debts, which fills almost a whole page.
