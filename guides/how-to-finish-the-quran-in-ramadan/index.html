How to finish the Quran in Ramadan

Read one juz a day, about 20 pages, or four pages after each of the five prayers. Here is a full 30-day plan, what to do if Ramadan has 29 days, and how to catch up if you fall behind.

The plan: one juz a day, which is about 20 pages and 40 minutes. The easiest way to fit it in is four pages after each of the five daily prayers, about eight minutes each time.

Why one juz a day works

The Quran is divided into 30 juz of roughly equal length, about 20 pages each in the standard 604-page mushaf. Ramadan is 29 or 30 days, so one juz a day finishes it almost exactly. Many mosques follow the same rhythm in taraweeh, so you can read the juz that will be recited that night.

Ramadan is the month the Quran was first revealed:

Al-Baqarah 2:185

شَهْرُ رَمَضَانَ ٱلَّذِىٓ أُنزِلَ فِيهِ ٱلْقُرْءَانُ هُدًۭى لِّلنَّاسِ وَبَيِّنَٰتٍۢ مِّنَ ٱلْهُدَىٰ وَٱلْفُرْقَانِ ۚ فَمَن شَهِدَ مِنكُمُ ٱلشَّهْرَ فَلْيَصُمْهُ ۖ وَمَن كَانَ مَرِيضًا أَوْ عَلَىٰ سَفَرٍۢ فَعِدَّةٌۭ مِّنْ أَيَّامٍ أُخَرَ ۗ يُرِيدُ ٱللَّهُ بِكُمُ ٱلْيُسْرَ وَلَا يُرِيدُ بِكُمُ ٱلْعُسْرَ وَلِتُكْمِلُوا۟ ٱلْعِدَّةَ وَلِتُكَبِّرُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ عَلَىٰ مَا هَدَىٰكُمْ وَلَعَلَّكُمْ تَشْكُرُونَ

Shahru Ramadaanallazeee unzila feehil Qur'aanu hudal linnaasi wa baiyinaatim minal hudaa wal furqaan; faman shahida minkumush shahra falyasumhu wa man kaana mareedan aw 'alaa safarin fa'iddatum min ayyaamin ukhar; yureedul laahu bikumul yusra wa laa yureedu bikumul 'usra wa litukmilul 'iddata wa litukabbirul laaha 'alaa maa hadaakum wa la'allakum tashkuroon

The month of Ramadhan [is that] in which was revealed the Qur'an, a guidance for the people and clear proofs of guidance and criterion. So whoever sights [the new moon of] the month, let him fast it; and whoever is ill or on a journey - then an equal number of other days. Allah intends for you ease and does not intend for you hardship and [wants] for you to complete the period and to glorify Allah for that [to] which He has guided you; and perhaps you will be grateful.

Fitting 20 pages into a fasting day

Forty minutes in one sitting is hard on a fasting day. Splitting it around the prayers makes it feel small:

WhenPagesTime
After Fajr4~8 min
After Dhuhr4~8 min
After Asr4~8 min
After Maghrib4~8 min
After Isha4~8 min

Prefer one longer session? After Fajr, before the day gets busy, is when many people find it easiest. For a day-by-day table with the exact page and ayah each day starts on, use the khatam planner.

If Ramadan has 29 days

You will not know until the moon is sighted, so plan for 29: about 21 pages a day. Or keep to one juz a day and read the last juz on the 29th alongside the 29th juz. It is a short one: Juz ‘Amma is made of short surahs.

Two khatams, or more

  • Twice in Ramadan: two juz a day, about 40 pages, or eight pages after each prayer.
  • Three times: three juz a day, about 60 pages, roughly two hours.

Choose what you can keep up for the whole month. A khatam read with attention is better than two rushed ones you abandon in the second week.

If you fall behind

Almost everyone does, usually around the middle of the month. Don’t try to read five juz in one night. Instead, take the pages you have left and spread them over the days remaining. On the planner, set “Starting from” to the juz you are on and “Finish in” to the days left.

Read with meaning

If you don’t read Arabic fluently, reading a translation alongside, or instead, is still time with the Quran. Even reading a few ayahs with full attention can stay with you for the whole day. The Quran verses about Ramadan and fasting are a good place to begin.
