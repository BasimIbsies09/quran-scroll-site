How long does it take to read the whole Quran?

About 15 to 20 hours for a fluent reader reciting the Arabic, and 25 to 40 hours or more if you are still learning. Here is how that breaks down per page, per juz and per day.

Short answer: about 15 to 20 hours of reciting for a fluent reader, at around two minutes per page. That is 40 minutes a day to finish in a month, or about four minutes a day to finish in a year.

The numbers

The standard Madani mushaf, the printed Quran most people read from, has 604 pages, 30 juz of about 20 pages each, 114 surahs and 6,236 ayahs. How long it takes depends almost entirely on how fluently you read Arabic.

ReaderPer pagePer juzWhole Quran
Fluent, steady pace1.5 min30 min~15 hours
Average2 min40 min~20 hours
Still learning3–4 min60–80 min30–40 hours
English translation only–~25 min~12 hours

The English figure is the Saheeh International translation, about 159,000 words, at 220 words a minute.

How much is that per day?

Spread over a month, 20 hours is 40 minutes a day: one juz. Over a year it is under four minutes a day, less time than most of us spend on a single scroll through social media. Use the khatam planner to work it out for your own date and pace, including how many pages to read after each prayer.

  • In 30 days (Ramadan): about 20 pages, or 40 minutes, a day.
  • In 60 days: about 10 pages, or 20 minutes, a day.
  • In a year: under 2 pages, or 3 to 4 minutes, a day.

Short surahs to start with

If the whole Quran feels like too much, start with a single surah. A few that many people begin with, with the time they take to recite:

  • Surah Al-Fatihah: about a minute
  • Surah Ya-Sin: about 11 minutes
  • Surah Al-Mulk: about 5 minutes
  • Surah Al-Kahf: about 24 minutes
  • Surah Ar-Rahman: about 6 minutes

Slow is not a problem

Reading slowly, with understanding, is something the Quran itself asks for. It describes itself as revealed in stages so that it could be recited “to the people over a prolonged period” (17:106), and asks “Then do they not reflect upon the Quran?” (4:82). Consistency matters more than speed: the Prophet ﷺ said the most beloved deeds to Allah are those done consistently, even if they are small (Bukhari and Muslim).

Al-Muzzammil 73:4

أَوْ زِدْ عَلَيْهِ وَرَتِّلِ ٱلْقُرْءَانَ تَرْتِيلًا

Aw zid 'alaihi wa rattilil Qur'aana tarteela

Or add to it, and recite the Qur'an with measured recitation.
